04 / CONDITIONAL RISKS

The questions that can change the deal.

If a meaningful portion of a site lies within regulated flood hazard areas: do not assume all listed acreage can support proposed improvements. Determine which areas are affected and which uses or construction types may require permits or restrictions.

If only disconnected pockets are reasonably flat: a nominally large parcel may not accommodate the arena, barns, vehicle circulation, and parking together. Acreage alone cannot establish fit.

If equestrian use is allowed but public events are not: the revenue model may change even if barns and paddocks are feasible. Ask the relevant local jurisdiction rather than assuming an agricultural designation solves everything.

05 / SOURCE PLAN, NOT A COMPLETED SEARCH

Where real evidence comes from.

In a real assignment, findings are linked to parcel-specific records and dated. These are general starting points, not sources used to verify the fictional parcel.

  • FEMA Flood Map Service Center — mapped flood hazard screening
  • USFWS National Wetlands Inventory — initial wetland screening
  • USDA Web Soil Survey — mapped soil resources
  • Appropriate county assessor, GIS, zoning, planning and land-use offices — parcel-specific confirmation

Coverage varies by jurisdiction. Public map layers may be out of date, incomplete, or unsuitable for engineering decisions.

06 / RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS

Don't confuse a screen with site approval.

  1. Obtain the actual parcel identifiers, current listing information, and surveyed boundaries when available.
  2. Screen flood, wetlands, terrain, and soils using dated parcel-specific sources.
  3. Confirm proposed equestrian/event uses directly with local planning and zoning officials.
  4. Have qualified professionals assess physical layouts, access, drainage, utilities and any permits before relying on buildability.

Deliverable boundary: desktop research can reveal what merits investigation and document evidence gaps. It does not guarantee buildable acreage, permitting, development costs, market value or feasibility.
