# Proposed editorial and commercial independence policy

Status: draft product policy; obtain qualified media/legal advice before publishing consequential allegations. This is not legal advice or a promise of immunity.

## Allegiance

Investigate institutions through evidence rather than a predetermined conclusion. Apply the same inclusion and review rules across parties, countries, and organizations. Publish null findings and cases that contradict the original hypothesis. Describe lawful but consequential incentives without relabeling them as crimes.

## No paid suppression

Customers may purchase workflow, licensed access, and research functionality. They may not purchase a more favorable score, silence, removal of an accurate finding, privileged editorial treatment, or a withheld correction. Sponsors and investors get no subject-specific veto. There must be no payment-conditioned demand to a subject of an investigation.

Corrections, lawful privacy protection, and legal compliance remain necessary. Do not promise permanent publication regardless of error or harm. Keep a public correction/takedown explanation where lawful and safe, without republishing protected information.

## Publication review

Every consequential claim needs a clear public-interest rationale, verified supporting records, an active search for counterevidence, explicit uncertainties, and human review. Use a second independent review for reputationally significant findings and qualified legal review as appropriate. Publish a subject's substantive response alongside the claim; lack of a response does not prove guilt. Contacting subjects is a separate authorized workflow and is not implemented here.

Clearly attribute allegations, official claims, findings, and judgments. Do not collapse suspicion, charge, or a numerical anomaly into guilt. Ordinary policy disagreement, coalition bargaining, legal lobbying, and resource-constrained journalism are viable alternative explanations that must not be excluded in advance.

## Privacy and source handling

Keep personal addresses, contact details, family information, and irrelevant sensitive material out of the graph and public exports. Focus on official acts and institutional relationships. Do not infer an individual's political affiliation from a donor record or use such records to create commercial targeting lists.

Do not encourage unauthorized access, acquire stolen credentials, bypass access controls, or publish raw private communications by default. A future confidential-source program requires dedicated legal, operational, and security design; a generic upload box must not be sold as secure whistleblower infrastructure.

## Corrections and appeals

Every dossier needs an accessible challenge route. Preserve the original method/result version internally with appropriate access controls, attach corrected evidence, and show what changed. Propagate material corrections to shared cards and exported manifests when possible. Fund the correction process independently of whether a subject is a customer.

## Editorial transparency

Publish methodology, source scope, coverage gaps, funding relationships, conflict-of-interest handling, review standards, and correction statistics. Do not publish a graph merely because it looks incriminating. A short understandable evidence chain is preferable to an unexplained web of associations.

The success measure is a reproducible public-interest finding that survives scrutiny, not outrage volume, threats, or takedown offers.
