# Product vision: a public-interest investigative workbench

Status: proposed capabilities, not shipped features or verified real-world findings.

## What makes this more than aggregation

The valuable unit is not an article count, an AI summary, or a scary network chart. It is a defensible answer to a question that otherwise requires a researcher to connect numerous separate records.

A proposed investigation should answer: What was promised? What exactly changed? Who publicly requested that change? Which entity is documented as introducing it? Who is eligible to benefit? Who actually received a benefit? How was it described? What remains unknown?

Do not promise to identify every author of every provision. A provision's first observed appearance, its formal sponsor, and its actual drafter are distinct facts. Unknown authorship must be visible.

## 1. Bill X-Ray and the legal before/after

Select an exact legislative artifact, not just a bill's short title. Display its status, version code, public law association where applicable, retrieval time, and available predecessor versions.

Create a complete section tree with links to original text, topic labels, plain-language effects, statutory cross-references, appropriations versus authorizations, eligibility rules, exceptions, effective dates, sunsets, and unresolved references. An unparsed section stays visible as unparsed rather than disappearing from the denominator.

A particularly valuable feature is a **legal effect diff**. Reconstruct the relevant before-and-after statutory wording where possible. Highlight changed numbers, definitions, deadlines, exemptions, enforcement powers, reporting obligations, and who qualifies. A short exception can matter more than hundreds of routine pages. Do not claim to compute the complete legal effect when incorporated law, regulations, litigation, or missing versions remain unresolved.

Compare headlines, article bodies, official messaging, and bill titles separately. Show which provisions each actually discusses, what it contradicts, and what it omits within a defined sample. Mere omission is not a false statement.

## 2. Policy lineage and first appearance

Track provisions across versions and across bills, including proposals that fail and later reappear in a larger package. Detect exact text matches, distinctive near-matches, and plausible semantic matches separately. Discount boilerplate.

Extend the lineage to publicly available industry submissions, regulatory comments, testimony, model bills, agency proposals, and lobbying disclosures when access and rights permit. A dated match is a research lead. It does not identify a secret author or prove copying, access, influence, or wrongdoing.

The interface should label a connection as recorded, inferred, contested, or unknown, with the exact evidence attached. Show alternative sources for the same language.

## 3. Promise Ledger: action is not outcome

Archive the original promise with date, speaker, office sought, issue, condition, deadline, transcript context, and measurable interpretation. Compare both supportive and contrary actions. Preserve amendments, procedural motions, final passage votes, sponsorship, member explanations, and reasons for nonparticipation where documented.

Maintain two separate tracks: **conduct relative to the commitment** and **policy outcome**. Someone can vote consistently with a promise and fail to achieve it; a policy can succeed without that person's support. An omnibus vote is not an unqualified endorsement of every provision. Sponsor, cosponsor, committee member, and leader are different roles.

Statuses: aligned action, contrary action, mixed record, outcome achieved, outcome not achieved, pending, no observed opportunity, ambiguous. Human-reviewed explanations should accompany any simplified public label.

## 4. Documented interests, potential beneficiaries, and actual outcomes

Connect organizations to disclosed lobbying issues, filed public comments, institutional campaign-finance records, eligibility definitions, rules, and spending awards. Keep organizations, subsidiaries, trade associations, PACs, and individuals separate unless an explicit sourced relationship connects them.

Never describe employees' donations as a company's donation. Never assign a filer's entire quarterly lobbying amount to a single bill unless the record explicitly supports it. Neither a contribution nor a financial benefit proves a purchased vote.

Use three beneficiary levels: directly named; rule-eligible under explicit assumptions; observed recipient. Label each level and include losers, taxpayer costs, distributional effects, and uncertainty where evidence supports them.

Extend the timeline beyond enactment: agency rules, grant eligibility, contract awards, program execution, enforcement, and measured outcomes. Authorization, appropriation, obligation, and outlay remain separate. Actual awards alone do not prove a law caused those awards.

## 5. Media Observatory and narrative provenance

Preserve the global violence/coverage concept, with defined event scope, independent event discovery, multilingual evidence, temporal U.S. relationship classification, and coverage completeness labels. Measure topic attention, unique editorial work, distribution through syndication, persistence, attribution, victim naming, and source reliance as distinct dimensions.

Add **narrative provenance**: trace when a claim first appeared in the monitored corpus, who was cited, and which stories reused it. Ten outlets repeating a single press release are ten distribution points, not ten independent confirmations. Do not claim to identify the absolute origin when the corpus is incomplete.

A government's relationship to the U.S. and an alleged perpetrator's relationship to that government are separate. An atrocity occurring in an allied country is not automatically an atrocity committed by an ally.

## 6. Investigation discovery and evidence packets

Propose leads from explicit signals: a consequential late eligibility change; unusual textual similarity; a promise/action tension; a large outcome/coverage mismatch; or a heavily repeated claim with little independent sourcing.

Rank **research priority**, not corruption. Expose each component: potential consequence, observable novelty, evidence quality, data gaps, and need for human review. The model must also search for ordinary explanations and contrary cases.

Each reviewed dossier has a persistent ID, concise finding, evidence timeline, original passages, query window, comparison set, analyst notes, contrary evidence, subject response, source rights, and correction history. Reproduce it from a versioned manifest wherever rights permit. Private licensed source content is not automatically included in public exports.

## Osiris experience

Osiris should ask and answer: What can we verify? What would falsify this inference? What context changes the interpretation? Which records would close the gap? A guided lesson can let a user compare a headline and provision before revealing the analysis. Reward careful reading and uncertainty calibration, not anger or political agreement.

Proposed tools: `explain_provision`, `compare_versions`, `trace_evidence`, `compare_promise_action`, `compare_coverage`, and `build_dossier`. These names describe future product interfaces, not integrations already implemented.

## Commercial hypothesis

Keep source-backed public findings, methodology, corrections, and responses openly readable. Charge for time-saving workflow: saved investigations, monitored topics, version alerts, collaborative annotation, evidence export, and licensed research integrations. Initial customers to interview are investigative reporters, small newsrooms, research groups, and civic organizations.

A possible discovery experiment is a paid newsroom pilot priced by documented research time saved. Pricing is unvalidated; no recurring revenue or willingness to pay is established. Do not sell individual-donor prospecting, source content without rights, suppression of findings, or preferential ratings. Respect the existing repository's no-live-billing readiness policy.

The moat we aim to build is the historical record, accurate entity/provision matching, adjudicated evidence, reproducibility, and trust. It is not a claim that no competing product exists.
