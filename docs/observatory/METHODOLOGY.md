# Methodology v0.1 (draft)

These are proposed research rules. No aggregate result or empirical conclusion is established by this document.

## Evidence before interpretation

Every graph edge is a claim, with a source snapshot, exact locator, source date where available, retrieval time, method version, confidence assessment, limitations, and review state. Store supporting, challenging, and contextual citations distinctly. A link merely mentioning two entities is not sufficient support for an alleged relationship.

Distinguish a recorded fact (a filing says X), an inference (X likely implies Y under assumptions), and a hypothesis (Y merits investigation). A recorded statement is evidence that it was said, not necessarily that its substance is true. A court allegation, a legal finding, and a conviction are different statuses.

A SHA-256 hash detects changes to a particular byte sequence; it does not prove truth, authenticity, or the original publication time. Preserve source copies only within applicable rights and privacy constraints. Keep publication timestamps with their precision; do not invent hour/minute values from dates.

## Bill X-Ray

Use the exact bill number, Congress, artifact/version code, and legislative status. Keep chamber-passed, enrolled, enacted, and effective separate. Match votes to the version and question actually voted upon, including amendments in the nature of a substitute where applicable. Prefer structured text, but preserve the authoritative original and explicitly mark parsing gaps.

Enumerate every section. A completeness manifest must include extracted, cross-referenced, unparsed, and unresolved items, with counts reconciling to the original tree. Incorporated material does not become a fully analyzed provision just because a cross-reference was detected.

Show topic composition under separate denominators:

- Word or text share measures textual footprint, not importance. Explain whether boilerplate and incorporated text are included.
- Provision share depends on the segmentation convention, which must be versioned and displayed.
- Scored budget effects require a cited score, time horizon, baseline, units, and correct sign. Do not add authorizations to appropriations, gross amounts to net effects, or nested account totals to suballocations. Keep CBO/JCT estimates separate from a model's estimate.
- Legal significance uses a reviewed rubric and cannot be reduced to dollars alone. Rights, enforcement, eligibility, and sunsets can be substantial without a reliable fiscal score.

Do not launch a universal "headline honesty" score. Start with a provision/headline/body evidence matrix. Distinguish supported, contradicted, partially supported, omitted, and not assessable. Fix an outlet sample and observation period before comparison; missing full text is not proof a topic was omitted.

For timestamps, label earliest observed availability in the monitored sources. Never present it as the actual release time unless independently supported. Compare substantive changes between versions, not PDF page count alone. Missing intermediate drafts can make apparent last-minute insertion misleading.

## Promise/action alignment

Record exact language, surrounding context, speaker, office, scope, strength, conditions, deadlines, and measurable interpretation before observing outcomes where feasible. Party platforms are not automatically personal commitments. Aspirations and binding commitments require distinct treatment.

Assess opportunity to act, relevant authority, procedural setting, and member explanations. Show mixed signals rather than forcing binary verdicts. A final-passage vote permits a narrow fact statement about the package, not a blanket stance on each provision. A failure of passage does not prove the member broke a promise to vote a particular way.

Do not publish a single politician ranking until promise extraction, opportunity coding, denominator choices, abstention handling, and reviewer agreement are validated. Missing evidence is not an adverse vote or broken promise.

## Media coverage and geopolitical comparisons

Define the event universe before querying U.S. coverage. Do not discover incidents only through the same U.S. outlets whose omissions are being studied. External event datasets also have selection biases, often rely partly on journalism, and do not create a complete census of real-world violence. Record shared underlying sources and perform sensitivity checks with non-U.S. and other documentation where feasible.

Start with a narrow event category with explicit inclusion rules. Political violence, terrorism, unlawful killings, and ordinary homicide are not interchangeable. Avoid legal labels such as war crime unless attributed to a competent source or determination.

Store casualty ranges and revisions, not only a single point estimate. Avoid identifying vulnerable victims unnecessarily. Separate alleged responsibility, confirmed responsibility, where the event occurred, and time-specific U.S. relations with relevant actors. Use treaty, sanctions, cooperation, and diplomatic dimensions with an unknown category; do not invent a definitive scalar ally/adversary score.

For each outlet and time interval, publish inclusion rules, ingestion health, coverage start, outages, retrieval limits, languages, event-matching uncertainty, and story count denominators. No observed match must display "not found in monitored sources/window," not "the media was silent."

Keep unique reporting/origin clusters, syndicated placements, article updates, word count, homepage prominence, and broadcast minutes separate. Do not infer homepage placement or airtime from article counts. A historic prominence metric requires a historic capture source. Normalize outlet attention by the outlet's total observed output when appropriate; a larger outlet's raw volume is not automatically greater emphasis.

An initial attention measure can be the share of an outlet's observed relevant output assigned to an event within a fixed interval. Count uncertainty and coverage holes explicitly. Per-fatality ratios are supplementary, unstable for small denominators, and not a measure of the value of a life.

Matched-event analyses should consider event category, casualty uncertainty, timing, American involvement, accessibility, language, novelty, conflict context, and available reporting. Do not choose only dramatic pairs. Predeclare inclusion rules and publish excluded and contrary examples.

A modeled residual is a discrepancy from a chosen baseline, not a normative verdict. Models can absorb the bias being studied. Compare multiple justified specifications, including and excluding geopolitics, use held-out evaluation, propagate uncertainty, and describe selection and measurement limits. Do not infer a causal effect of geopolitical relationships from an uncontrolled observational association. A before/after relationship change is not automatically a valid natural experiment.

## Influence and beneficiaries

Prefer stable institution identifiers and reviewed aliases. Preserve subsidiaries, parent relationships, date ranges, and uncertainty. Similar names are not identity proof. Do not reveal personal donor addresses or create individual political profiles for commercial targeting.

Keep campaign receipts, independent expenditures, lobbying expenses, legislative activity, and procurement awards in distinct ledgers. Account for amended filings, refunds, duplicates, lags, and organization mergers. Institutional or employee relationships require their own evidence.

Textual overlap suggests shared language, not authorship. Report exact overlap, date availability, baseline boilerplate frequency, and competing source documents. No direct evidence of authorship means the author is unknown.

Eligibility is not receipt. Receipt is not profit. Profit is not proof of corruption. Expose direct beneficiaries, rule-based potential beneficiaries, and observed recipients separately. Report plausible routine explanations and negative/control comparisons as well as suspicious patterns.

## Review and evaluation

Create a labeled evaluation set before publishing scores. Double-annotate a sample, adjudicate disagreements, and report precision/recall, missed provisions, unresolved references, entity-match false merges, citation correctness, and reviewer agreement by task and source class. Do not replace these with a model's self-rated confidence.

Proposed initial release criteria: every displayed factual claim has a working source locator; every section is accounted for including failures; no public sample relies on a known wrong entity merge; no important unsupported allegation remains; publication review is complete; and all prominently reported limits are visible. These are acceptance targets, not measured achievements.

Publish method versions, corpus manifests, sensitivity results, and known limitations. Revisions create a new result version. Subject responses and corrections must remain attached to shareable dossiers.
